How Coral Bleaching Occurs?

One of the reasons is accumulated heat stress, measured in Degree Heating Weeks (DHW). Corals have a mutualistic symbiotic relationship with zooxanthellae, which provide them with colors and nutrients through photosynthesis. When corals experience prolonged elevated sea temperatures, they expel zooxanthellae, leading to bleaching. DHW quantifies this stress by summing the intensity and duration of heat exposure over a 12-week period. Higher DHW values indicate greater risk of bleaching and coral mortality.
